Testowe: Finite-time blow-up versus global existence in a parabolic–elliptic Keller–Segel system with logistic damping

Mister Test (Uniwersytet Test)

We consider the parabolic–elliptic Keller–Segel system with a logistic source μu(1−u)\mu u(1-u)μu(1−u) in a bounded domain of Rn\mathbb{R}^nRn, n≥3n \ge 3n≥3. It is known that for μ\muμ large enough the logistic damping prevents aggregation and all solutions are global and bounded, whereas without it radially symmetric solutions with large mass concentrate in finite time. We show that in the intermediate regime the dichotomy is governed by a critical Morrey-type norm of the initial datum: below a threshold solutions exist globally, while for a class of radial data above it blow-up occurs in finite time, despite the damping. The proofs combine comparison arguments for the cumulated mass function with new weighted energy estimates.

Long time behaviour of mean field games with fractional diffusion

Espen Jakobsen (NTNU)
Streszczenie

We discuss recent results on the long time behaviour of mean field games systems with fractional diffusion. The dynamics of the players are driven by independent jump processes, controlled through the drift, and confined by an external drift to guarantee ergodicity. In the case of globally Lipschitz, locally uniformly convex Hamiltonians, and weakly coupled and LL monotone costs, we prove that there is a unique solution (uT,mT)(u_T,m_T)(uT​,mT​) to the problem in (0,T)(0,T)(0,T), and we show that if T is sufficiently large, then (uT,mT)(u_T,m_T)(uT​,mT​) satisfies the so-called turnpike property — it is exponentially close to the (unique) stationary ergodic state of the problem.

A Schauder regularity theory for nonlocal viscous Hamilton–Jacobi equations

Robin Østern Lien (NTNU)
Streszczenie

Viscous Hamilton–Jacobi equations arise as dynamic programming equations in optimal control and differential games, where the unknown function represents the value function of an optimally controlled process. We use a heat-semigroup approach to study the regularity of such equations with nonlocal and mixed local–nonlocal diffusions. Our framework covers a broad class of Lévy-type operators, including fractional Laplacians as well as nonsymmetric and strongly anisotropic operators. Using the Duhamel formulation together with smoothing estimates in Hölder spaces, we obtain optimal spatial Schauder estimates and explicit blow-up rates for higher-order Hölder norms as time approaches zero. We focus on two canonical regimes: Lipschitz initial data, leading to solutions with uniformly bounded gradients, and lower-regularity Hölder initial data, where gradients may blow up and their behavior is linked to the growth of the Hamiltonian. Under additional assumptions on the diffusion, we also obtain optimal regularity in time and joint space-time Schauder estimates consistent with the natural fractional parabolic scaling. We further discuss short- and long-time existence, uniqueness, and classical solutions. Our motivation comes from mean field games (MFGs), with the goal of applying this regularity theory to nonlocal MFGs with low-regularity data. The talk is based on our paper “A Schauder regularity theory for nonlocal and mixed local—nonlocal viscous Hamilton—Jacobi equations” (joint work with Espen R. Jakobsen and Artur Rutkowski).
