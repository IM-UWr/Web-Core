A guide for students starting bachelor’s studies at the Mathematical Institute: in mathematics, in artificial intelligence in data science (SIAD) and — for the mathematical part of the programme — in ISIM. The most important information is at the top; full details are in the Guide for new students (PDF, Polish), prepared by the year coordinator.

Announcements

The health and safety (BHP) training must be completed by 31 October 2026. It is an online course; the details were sent to you by e-mail.

  • Guide for new mathematics students (PDF, Polish) — required reading, please read it carefully.
  • First-semester timetable for mathematics — corrected version of 9 October 2026 (PDF). If in doubt, the most up-to-date (though less convenient) version is the winter-semester timetable.
  • The tutoring room has been open in room 202 since 19 October — the tutors’ schedule is attached to the announcement.
  • The winter exam timetable is available.
  • Accounts in the University systems (USOSweb and Office 365) have been created for you, and the login details were sent to the e-mail address you gave during admission. Please activate these accounts.

First steps

  1. Activate your USOSweb account — the activation e-mail was sent to the address you gave in the IRK admission system. USOSweb is the online student office: class groups, grades, course registration, decisions and payments.
  2. Activate your Office 365 account (e-mail in the uwr.edu.pl domain) — the second activation e-mail; details in USOSweb. Messages sent to this mailbox by USOS, the Faculty Office, the Institute’s management and lecturers are considered delivered — check it regularly or set up forwarding.
  3. Submit your oath and declaration in USOSweb. After logging in to your student account you submit, electronically, the student oath and a statement that you have read the basic documents on your studies. This is the basis for the Faculty Office to issue your student ID card. The Faculty Office sees the submitted oath only on the following day. Failing to submit the oath in USOS within 14 days of the start of the semester is grounds for removal from the list of students (§ 56 of the Study Regulations).
  4. Collect your student ID card at the Faculty Office (dziekanat WMI, Mon–Thu 11:00–14:00), bringing your identity card. This applies to those who paid for the card in time. Certificates of student status are also issued there.
  5. Complete the BHP training — an online course, available in 2026 from 15 September to 31 October. It is compulsory.
  6. Check your groups in USOSweb: Mój USOSweb → Grupy zajęciowe.
  7. Connect to eduroam Wi-Fi — login student_ID_number@uwr.edu.pl and your Office 365 password; instructions: Wireless network access.
  8. Get a library account — complete the library training (an online course and a test; the access key is given on the training page).
  9. Get a locker key from the porter’s desk. There is usually one locker for two students.

First-year courses

Semester 1 (mathematics)

The main courses are taught at two levels: standard and extended. In the first semester you are enrolled either in all three courses at the extended level or in all three at the standard level.

Common to everyone:

  • Wstęp do kombinatoryki (Introduction to combinatorics) — compulsory for everyone except students of the theoretical mathematics specialisation. Everyone is enrolled in it, but those who already declare that they plan to take the theoretical specialisation may drop it.
  • Warsztat informatyczny matematyka (Computer skills workshop) — teaches the basic tools for using the Institute’s computer network efficiently; held remotely in two groups.

The lectures Analiza matematyczna I and Algebra liniowa 1R are shared with ISIM students; mathematics students have separate exercise groups.

Semester 2 (mathematics)

  • Analiza matematyczna 2 or Analiza matematyczna II
  • Algebra liniowa 2 or Algebra liniowa 2R
  • Programowanie 1 (Python) — compulsory for everyone
  • Topologia or Topologia R — compulsory except for those who have declared the teaching specialisation (they have a different list of courses) and those who choose the general module (a humanities course is recommended for them, though they can also take Topology)
  • Przegląd aktuarialno-finansowy (actuarial and financial overview, as workshops) — compulsory for the actuarial and financial specialisation, an elective for everyone else

SIAD and ISIM

  • SIAD: in semesters 1–2 — mathematical analysis and linear algebra (in a version for this programme), introduction to mathematics, Python programming and data visualisation, and the basic AI workshop. Classes are in the Institute’s timetable (groups marked “tylko dla SIAD”). In the first semester you are enrolled by the teaching office. More: AI in Data Science.
  • ISIM: the compulsory courses are Logic for Computer Science and Analiza matematyczna I; recommended ones include Algebra liniowa 1R. In the first semester you register for the Institute’s courses yourself in USOSweb, and the tutors review your choices. Students admitted to both ISIM and mathematics did not take the qualifying test. More: ISIM.

Standard or extended level?

  • Standard level courses are more elementary: more examples are presented and less emphasis is placed on theory. Extended level courses treat the material more deeply, place more emphasis on theory and require you to absorb new material faster.
  • In the first semester you were assigned to a level on the basis of the qualifying test and the extended-level matura in mathematics (taking into account the declarations made during the test).
  • Switching from extended to standard is possible for each course separately — via form B3. The lecturers of both versions set the deadline and announce it by the end of the second week of classes; you have to decide at the latest after the first test in the extended course.
  • In semester 2 students almost always continue analysis and linear algebra at the same level. You can move to a less advanced level; a move in the other direction is very rare but allowed. A prerequisite of a standard course is also met by its extended version.
  • Students who are even considering the theoretical mathematics specialisation should take all courses at the extended level from the start (and Topology in semester 2). The curricula of the other specialisations (except teaching) are similar in semesters 1–4, but differ slightly in the choice of standard or extended versions of some courses — see the Guide to the bachelor’s curriculum (PDF, Polish).
  • In USOSweb you can be registered for only one level of a given course at a time.

Your first-semester level and group apply only to first-semester courses. They do not limit your choice of courses in later semesters or your choice of specialisation.

Course registration

Passing the first year (mathematics)

To pass a course you need a positive grade in every form of classes — usually the exercise classes and the exam. The rules below are set by the Vice-Dean on the basis of the curriculum and resolutions of the Faculty Council.

Students who pass the semester do not submit any applications. Students who meet the conditions for re-enrolment in semester 2 must submit an application for re-enrolment (DOC) at the Faculty Office — failing to do so counts as failing the semester and is grounds for removal from the list of students.

  • The re-enrolment rule does not apply to students who have failed a compulsory course twice or are taking semester 2 for the second time — they are usually removed from the list of students; in special cases the Dean considers a separate application individually.
  • A student repeating semester 2 may register for courses of later years, but not for any course whose prerequisite is a course they have failed.
  • When you retake a course, exercise classes or labs you have already passed remain valid — you attend only the lecture and take the exam (in USOS you register for group 99).
  • Cases not covered by these rules are decided individually by the Dean.

Documents: decisions for students finishing semester 1 (PDF), decisions for students finishing semester 2 (PDF). The deadline for applications and the rules for repeating a semester: Student affairs.

These decisions apply to the mathematics programme. On ISIM you need at least 27 ECTS and the compulsory courses to pass semester 1 (see ISIM); on SIAD the requirements are set by the programme’s curriculum — if in doubt, ask the Faculty Office.

Classes and how to study

  • Lecture — the lecturer presents the theory with examples; you listen, take notes and ask questions (this is welcome).
  • Exercise classes — solving problems from lists published online by the lecturer. You are expected to try to solve the problems before the class and to take an active part (at the board).
  • Konwersatorium — an extra hour at the lecturer’s disposal: a test for all groups, an additional exercise hour or extra material.
  • Lab — work in one of the computer labs.

In the first year the main courses (Analiza matematyczna 1, Algebra liniowa 1, Wstęp do matematyki) have frequent written tests (kolokwia). The tests are scored and the exercise-class grade is based on the total score (activity can only raise a grade that is already positive). The grading scale is 2, 3, 3.5, 4, 4.5, 5; 2 is a fail. In principle there is no procedure for retaking a single test — you simply have to do better in the next one (although some courses have a resit test at the end of the semester). Lecturers give the detailed rules at the beginning of the course.

Only students who have passed the exercise classes may take the exam in the exam session. If you fail it, you have a second attempt in the resit session (in winter right after the main session, in summer at the beginning of September). Failing the resit means failing the course.

Failing the same compulsory course twice usually results in removal from the list of students, and repeating a course has to be paid for.

The Institute does not use the week parity shown in USOS but day colours: for classes held every other week, one group meets on orange days and the other on blue days — see Day colours.

Advice from the guide:

  • Plan 20–25 hours a week of independent work — roughly as much as you spend in class.
  • Attend lectures and exercise classes (not only the tests), and come prepared: revise the definitions and theorems from the previous lecture and work on the problem list yourself before the class.
  • Aim to understand, not to memorise solutions — the question “why is it so?” matters more than “how do I do it?”. There are few routine methods in university mathematics.
  • If you get stuck, keep trying — an honest attempt teaches you more than copying someone else’s solution, and at worst you will know what to ask about.
  • Ask questions — in class, during office hours and in the tutoring room.
  • Be careful with AI tools: having a language model solve the problem list will not teach you mathematical thinking, and it will not help you in tests and exams.
  • Mathematics is learned in a spiral — first-year topics come back in later years, so the goal is understanding, not just passing the exam.

Worth knowing now: languages and specialisations

Foreign languages (mathematics)

  • Language courses start in semester 3 and are run by the School of Foreign Languages (SPNJO). They must end with an exam at level B2 II.
  • You choose the language yourself (English, French, Spanish, German, Russian or Italian). The chosen language becomes compulsory and cannot be changed later.
  • A placement test decides where you start: below B1 — you must make up the gap on your own; B2 I or B2 II — you start at that level; C1 I — you may be exempt from classes, but you must pass the final exam (B2 II).
  • The University provides three semesters of free tuition. Unused free hours can be used to learn another language.
  • You may be exempt from classes and/or the final exam on the basis of certain documents listed by SPNJO — this must be reported before the placement test.

The basic language of mathematical studies is English: the specialisations data analysis, applied mathematics, actuarial and financial mathematics and theoretical mathematics require English, and mathematics teaching — English or German. With a different choice you follow the general module and graduate without a named specialisation.

University rules: foreign language courses (BIP, Polish).

Specialisations (mathematics)

  • There are five specialisations: data analysis, applied mathematics, actuarial and financial mathematics, theoretical mathematics and mathematics teaching; you can also study without a specialisation (the general path).
  • You declare your specialisation by the end of semester 4 (form C2, by 30 June). The exception is mathematics teaching — you declare it by 31 December of semester 1 (form C1). You can change your declaration until the end of your studies.
  • It is worth preparing for the decision from the beginning of semester 2. To work as a teacher you need a master’s degree, so you have to complete both the bachelor’s and the master’s programme.

Details: Mathematics — specialisations and deadlines and forms.

FAQ

Which group am I in? Check USOSweb: Mój USOSweb → Grupy zajęciowe. In semester 1 mathematics and SIAD students are assigned to groups by the teaching office.

I am at the extended level and it is too hard. What can I do? You can switch to the standard version of each course separately via form B3 — by the deadline announced by the lecturers (at the latest after the first test). See Standard or extended level?

Does my first-semester level affect my choice of specialisation? No. The assignment applies only to first-semester courses.

What are orange and blue days? The Institute uses day colours instead of the week parity shown in USOS — see Day colours.

My test went badly. Can I retake it? In principle there are no retakes of individual tests — you have to do better in the next one. Some lecturers hold a resit test at the end of the semester; the rules are given at the beginning of the course.

Do I register for physical education and a language course in the first year? You do not register for physical education in the first semester. Language courses in the mathematics programme start in semester 3.

When do I choose my specialisation? By the end of semester 4; mathematics teaching — by 31 December of semester 1. See Specialisations.

Who should I write to when I do not know what to do? To the year coordinator, Dr Jan Kraszewski (jan.kraszewski@uwr.edu.pl). If he does not know the answer, he knows who does.
