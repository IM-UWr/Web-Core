A guide to classes at the Mathematical Institute: how and when to register for courses in USOSweb, which form to submit in special situations, and where to find the timetable, exam dates and lecturers’ office hours. Specialisation declarations and the choice of a thesis supervisor (forms C1–C3 and E) are described on the programme page: Mathematics — deadlines and forms.

  • Timetable: winter semester 2026/27, summer semester 2026/27. Together with the timetable, the full list of courses offered in the semester (by category, including one-off courses) is published before the semester starts.
  • Day colours: classes held every other week follow the day colours, not the week parity shown in USOS — see Day colours.

Office hours

Every member of staff who teaches sets aside time each week to meet students — two hours a week (PhD students: one hour). This is the best place to ask about material you do not understand: go to your lecturer or class teacher (you may also go to any other member of staff) and prepare specific questions beforehand.

The list of office hours is published for each semester, separately for staff and for PhD students. For each person it gives the day, the hours and the room, plus remarks — for example that office hours can also be held remotely on MS Teams or at another time arranged by e-mail. The list is also posted on the noticeboard next to the Institute office (level 500 of the Institute building).

The list of office hours for the winter semester 2026/27 (staff and PhD students) will be posted here once it is published.

Until then:

  • e-mail addresses and room numbers of staff and PhD students are in the staff directory; many entries also link to a personal website, where it is worth checking for office hours;
  • also check the lecturer’s profile in USOSweb;
  • if in doubt, ask the lecturer in class or arrange a meeting by e-mail.

Course registration

You register for Mathematical Institute courses yourself in USOSweb. Exceptions:

  • 1st-semester bachelor’s students of mathematics and artificial intelligence in data analysis (SIAD) are enrolled by the Institute’s teaching office (1st-semester ISIM students register themselves);
  • separate registration systems apply to e-learning courses run with Hasselt University (forms H1 and H2), courses of the Institute of Computer Science (its own registration system), foreign language courses (Foreign Language Centre) and physical education (University Centre for Physical Education and Sport) — details below.

Full rules: Rules of course registration at the Mathematical Institute (PDF, Polish).

Registration rounds

Registration opens 2–4 days before the semester starts and works on a first-come, first-served basis. Exact dates of each round: registration schedule in USOSweb.

RoundWhenWhat you can do
1st (with queues)1–2 days, ends before classes startjoin a queue for a group; when the round ends, the system enrols people from the front of the queue until the limit is reached and rejects the remaining requests
2nd (no queues)first two weeks of the semesterregister directly in groups with free places and withdraw freely
3rd (no queues)1–2 daysonly register for free places — withdrawal is no longer possible

Note: if your request for one form of classes is rejected, your request for the whole course is rejected — e.g. no place in a lab group also means rejection from the tutorial group. Once the 2nd round has closed, you can no longer withdraw from a course except in the cases listed under Forms.

Group limits

Every group has a limit of places, set by the room size, the form of classes and an even split of students between groups. If a course is in high demand, the limit may be raised or an extra group created, where possible. In exceptional situations you may submit a request, with detailed reasons, to join a group above its limit — Form B2 (available from the start of the 2nd round).

Changes appear in USOSweb the next day

USOS (used by the Faculty Office and the teaching office) and USOSweb (for students) have separate databases, synchronised once a day, around midnight. Therefore:

  • changes made by the teaching office (new limits, an extra group, a new class time, adding a student manually) appear in USOSweb only the next day;
  • the teaching office sees up-to-date lists of students registered through USOSweb only after the overnight synchronisation.

Teacher-training courses

Teacher-training courses (category N) have a separate registration in USOSweb, open only to students who have declared the mathematics teaching specialisation on Form C1 or C3 (deadlines).

Good practice

  • Register only for classes you really intend to attend — not for courses you have already passed or whose prerequisites you do not meet (unless you have permission).
  • For courses taught at two levels you may be registered for only one level at a time.
  • If you decide to drop a course, withdraw at once instead of waiting for registration to end — especially for classes with a limited number of places.

Prerequisites

A course’s prerequisites are listed in USOS, in the “Założenia” section of the syllabus. Note that:

  • a requirement to have completed the standard version of a course is also met by its extended version;
  • a requirement to have completed Podstawy statystyki praktycznej is also met by Statystyka or Podstawy statystyki.

If you do not meet the prerequisites, in exceptional cases you can still take the course:

  • bachelor’s students — with the consent of the Deputy Director for Teaching: before the semester starts (in the winter semester by 1 October) submit Form B1 and, after a positive decision, register yourself;
  • master’s students — with the lecturer’s consent: once you have it, register yourself, without submitting a request.

Graduates of other universities who are unsure whether they meet the prerequisites should consult the lecturer, who takes the final decision.

Standard and extended versions

The key compulsory courses of the first semesters are taught at two levels: a standard and an extended version (marked with the letter R or a Roman numeral, e.g. Algebra liniowa 1R, Analiza matematyczna I). Both cover a comparable range of material, but the extended version treats it in more depth — with complete proofs of most theorems and more emphasis on harder problems.

  • 1st semester (mathematics): students are assigned to a version on the basis of their secondary-school final exam results and the qualification test, also taking into account the preferences given during the test.
  • From the 2nd semester: you choose the version yourself, separately for each course. Choosing the extended version of a course in the Analiza matematyczna or Algebra liniowa sequence after completing the standard version of the previous course requires consulting the lecturer of the extended version first.
  • Choosing the standard version of the compulsory courses of semesters 1–3 does not close any path, but the standard version of some later courses may limit your choice of specialisation. Recommendations of the specialisation coordinators: Table 7 in the Guide to the bachelor’s curriculum (PDF, Polish) (curriculum from 2026/27).
  • You cannot receive ECTS credits for both versions of the same course.

Switching from the extended to the standard version

A student of mathematics or ISIM may switch from the extended to the standard version of a compulsory course even after registration has closed: submit Form B3 within the period agreed by the lecturers of both versions and announced by the end of the second week of classes. This switch does not use up the one-time right to withdraw from a course (A1/A2).

Withdrawing after registration has closed

Once the 2nd round has closed, you can withdraw from a course only in three situations:

  1. switching from the extended to the standard version — Form B3 (see above);
  2. withdrawal from an e-learning course run with Hasselt University — Form H2 (see E-learning);
  3. one-time withdrawal from a course — once per degree (once in the bachelor’s and once in the master’s programme), by 30 November (winter semester) or 30 April (summer semester): Form A1 (bachelor’s) or A2 (master’s). For courses with a team project (e.g. Zespołowy projekt specjalnościowy) the lecturer’s consent is also required. This right is independent of the corresponding right at the Institute of Computer Science.

Repeating a course

  • A student who has failed a compulsory course must register for it again at the first opportunity; repeating a course is subject to a fee.
  • Tutorials or labs you have already passed remain valid: if you failed only the exam, after registering again you attend only the lecture and take the exam. Because USOS requires registration for all forms of classes, register for group no. 99 in that case. For courses with both tutorials and labs, this rule applies to each of them separately.
  • The rule does not apply to a new course of study (e.g. after re-admission or in the master’s programme). If you are unsure whether a credit is still valid, contact the Faculty Office.
  • For a course taught at two levels, you may choose the version of the course you repeat (the standard version is recommended).

Exam session

In the academic year 2026/27 the winter exam session runs from 6 to 16 February 2027 and the resit session from 17 to 23 February 2027; in the summer semester — from 18 June to 1 July 2027 and from 1 to 9 September 2027 (Academic year).

Exam dates for lectures

Exam dates for lectures for students in semesters 1–3 of the bachelor’s programmes in mathematics, SIAD and ISIM are announced in the first half of October:

  • main session schedule, February 2027 (PDF)
  • resit session schedule, February 2027 (PDF)

Exam dates for lectures not included in these schedules are announced by the lecturer. The schedules also give the dates of the written bachelor’s and master’s exams — more on Exams and diploma.

Key rules (Study Regulations)

  • Within 14 days of the start of classes the teacher announces the form of assessment and the requirements (§ 20).
  • Only students who have passed the other forms of classes (tutorials, labs) are admitted to the exam. Classes that do not end with an exam are credited before the session starts (§ 31).
  • You have the right to two attempts: the main one in the exam session and a resit in the resit session. If you pass at the first attempt, you cannot take the resit. An earlier exam date is possible on terms set by the examiner (§ 34).
  • If your absence from an exam is excused, the examiner sets a new date; with the Dean’s consent exams may be held up to 10 days after the end of the session (§ 34).
  • The curriculum may include at most 8 exams per academic year and at most 5 per session (§ 30).
  • Check your grades in USOSweb and report a wrong or missing grade to the teacher within 3 working days. Appeals and other procedures: Student affairs.

Other institutes and faculties

Institute of Computer Science

Mathematics students may register for courses of the Institute of Computer Science if places are available — check the Institute of Computer Science registration system. You need an account in that system: write to zapisy@cs.uni.wroc.pl with a copy to the Deputy Director for Teaching of the Institute of Computer Science (filip.chudy@cs.uni.wroc.pl), giving your full name, student ID number, programme and semester of study.

Whether a course of the Institute of Computer Science counts towards the mathematical electives (category L or M) is decided by the Dean.

Other faculties

You may take a course taught at another faculty of the University and count it towards your studies with the consent of the Dean of the Faculty of Mathematics and Computer Science and of the dean of the faculty offering the course (contact that faculty’s office). This applies in particular to the humanities or social sciences elective and, in the master’s programme, to courses in the foundations of the natural or exact sciences. The Dean decides how the course is included in your study plan; courses counted as extracurricular are not included in the settlement of your studies but are recorded in the diploma supplement (§ 28 of the Study Regulations). Request forms: Request forms (Faculty, Polish).

Language courses and PE

Foreign language courses

Language courses are taught by the Foreign Language Centre (SPNJO), which also runs the registration. For the curriculum from 2026/27:

  • Bachelor’s: the language course starts in the 3rd semester and ends with an exam at level B2 II. Before the 3rd semester you must take the qualification test at the time and place announced by SPNJO — the test determines the level you start at.
  • Choice of language: all specialisations except mathematics teaching require English; mathematics teaching — English or German; the general path (no specialisation) — any language offered by SPNJO. You cannot change the chosen language during the course.
  • Exemptions: the language certificates that qualify for an exemption are listed in the annex to the regulations on language courses; report the exemption to SPNJO before the qualification test.
  • Master’s: the curriculum includes a language course (60 hours, ending with an exam, 4 ECTS) — the semester is given in the Guide to the master’s curriculum (PDF, Polish).

Rules: language courses — new curriculum (Rector’s order no. 138/2026, Polish), language courses — previous curriculum (Rector’s order no. 42/2020, Polish).

Physical education

  • The bachelor’s programme includes two semesters of compulsory physical education, which you may take in any semesters. Students starting their studies do not register for PE in the 1st semester.
  • Classes and registration are run by the University Centre for Physical Education and Sport (UCWFS). Registration is electronic, in USOS (token-based registration); its schedule is published in USOS a week before registration. It is followed by a correction period, when you can change the type of classes if places are available.
  • The grade depends on attendance: four absences not made up mean failing the classes. You must make up an absence within three weeks of returning to classes (no more than 4 make-ups per semester).
  • A medical exemption from physical activity does not exempt you from PE — you then take theory classes or the online health and sports education course.
  • Some classes are partly paid; the fee is due within 14 days of the end of the correction period, and if you do not withdraw during the correction period you are charged even if you do not attend.
  • Rules: regulations on PE classes and assessment (Rector’s order no. 148/2026, Polish).
