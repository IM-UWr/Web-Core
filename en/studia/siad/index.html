About

Artificial Intelligence in Data Science is a three-year (six-semester), full-time, general academic bachelor’s programme run by the Mathematical Institute at the Faculty of Mathematics and Computer Science, University of Wrocław. It was launched in the 2025/26 academic year in response to labour-market demand.

Its aim is to prepare students to solve complex practical problems based on large data sets using artificial intelligence methods and tools. The programme combines solid foundations in mathematics and computer science (71% of ECTS credits in mathematics, 29% in computer science) with practice. You will learn:

  • Python programming,
  • machine learning and neural networks,
  • data mining and dimensionality reduction techniques,
  • cloud computing,
  • multivariable mathematical analysis,
  • linear algebra,
  • statistical inference.

Studies are taught in Polish and lead to the licencjat (bachelor’s) degree.

A comprehensive curriculum Solid foundations in mathematics and computer science, so that you understand the algorithms you use and can develop AI tools further.
Specialist skills Machine learning algorithms, neural network architectures, data analysis and visualisation, training networks on GPUs, clusters and in the cloud.
Practice and team projects Lab classes and larger team projects in which you analyse data, build models and implement AI algorithms.

How the studies work

Bachelor's studies

  1. Semesters 1–2 Foundations of mathematics and programming Mathematical analysis and linear algebra (in a version for this programme), introduction to mathematics, Python programming and data visualisation. The basic AI workshop covers working in Linux, with repositories and version control, and with basic AI tools.
  2. Semesters 3–4 Statistics and machine learning Probability, graphs and algorithms, foundations of statistics, machine learning and further Python programming. The advanced AI workshop covers containers, machine learning pipelines and cloud services, among other things. The first electives in mathematics, computer science and programming begin.
  3. Semesters 5–6 Neural networks, project and diploma Neural networks, recent advances and applications of AI, and electives, including data analysis. In the 6th semester, a team project — solving a problem posed by non-specialists with AI tools — and the diploma exam.

After graduation

Graduates have up-to-date knowledge of methods and tools for data analysis using artificial intelligence, built on a thorough grounding in mathematics and computer science. They can create AI-based services, solve practical data analysis problems, and automate and streamline operations in various industries.

Example roles after graduation: data analyst, machine learning engineer, data engineer, AI researcher, AI consultant, AI start-up founder.

The Faculty of Mathematics and Computer Science also runs Artificial Intelligence, a master’s programme taught jointly by the Institute of Computer Science and the Mathematical Institute.

FAQ

How do electives work?

Electives fall into three categories: mathematics and computer science courses, data analysis, and programming (courses in the last two categories also count towards the first). In total you need at least 53 ECTS credits from them, including at least 6 ECTS in data analysis and at least 5 ECTS in programming. The full list of electives is announced before each semester. You may take them in a different order than in the study plan, provided that at the end of each semester you have no fewer ECTS credits than the programme requires.

Does the programme include work placements?

No — the curriculum does not include work placements.

How do the studies end?

According to the curriculum, in the 6th semester you complete a team project and take the diploma exam. Graduates are awarded the licencjat (bachelor’s) degree.

Where can I find the timetable?

Classes are listed in the Mathematical Institute timetable (groups marked “tylko dla SIAD”). Registration rules and forms are on the Classes and office hours page.

Where can I get help in my first year?

From the first-year coordinator and on the First year page, which includes the orientation day programme for this degree.
