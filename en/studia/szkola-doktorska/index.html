About the doctoral school

The Doctoral College of Mathematics is part of the Doctoral School of the University of Wrocław and operates at the Faculty of Mathematics and Computer Science. Its aim is to prepare doctoral students to write a doctoral thesis and to carry out independent research and teaching in mathematics.

Training lasts 8 semesters and follows an individual research plan and individual course plans agreed with the supervisor and approved by the head of the College. It leads to learning outcomes at level 8 of the Polish Qualifications Framework and ends with the submission of the doctoral thesis.

Courses

The compulsory course load is 270 hours. Within the courses run by the College you should complete at least 4 lecture courses of at least 30 hours each, ending with an exam, and at least 2 semester-long seminars of 30 hours. You can also count university-wide courses for all doctoral colleges, courses offered by the Faculty (including research seminars), individual courses outside the regular offer, courses at other faculties and universities, and professional practice. With the consent of the head of the College you can also take courses offered by the Doctoral College of Computer Science. Classes are taught in Polish or English. Syllabi of recent courses are on the Documents and forms page.

What the programme expects of you

  • active participation in scientific conferences and other research events,
  • demonstrating the ability to prepare a research grant application,
  • office hours of 1 hour a week,
  • up to 10 hours a year of organisational work for the Faculty of Mathematics and Computer Science, e.g. helping to organise conferences, Open Days and outreach events, taking part in the Lower Silesian Science Festival, or helping with exams.

Accounts to set up at the start

Within 14 days of starting at the doctoral school, each doctoral student must:

  • create an individual account in the Polish Scholarly Bibliography (PBN),
  • link the PBN account with their ORCID identifier,
  • link the PBN account with their identifier in the POLON system.

People and defences

The Institute’s doctoral students are listed in the staff directory. Defence notices, theses and reviews are published on the PhD defences page. All doctoral students of the University form the Doctoral Students’ Council of the University of Wrocław.

A+ category Mathematics at the University of Wrocław holds the A+ research category — you prepare your doctorate among active researchers.
Guidance of a supervisor Your supervisor (a habilitated doctor or a professor) supports you throughout your training; together you set your research plan and individual course plan.
Doctoral stipend A doctoral student without a doctoral degree receives a stipend — at least 37% of a professor's salary, and at least 57% after the mid-term evaluation.
Teaching experience Professional practice (up to 60 hours a year) prepares you to work as an academic teacher — you teach students or assist in teaching.
Research visits and mobility With the consent of the head of the College you can do research visits at other institutions, including abroad — time spent abroad counts towards your training.
A place at the Institute Doctoral students have a workplace at the Mathematical Institute and access to its research infrastructure; they can receive travel funding and research grants.

The doctoral path

Doctoral training

  1. Before you start Admission Find a prospective supervisor, register in the IRKa system and take the written entrance exam (12 problems; the 6 best solutions are graded). In recent years in June (2026 — exam on 17 June) IRKa
  2. First month Oath, supervisor and research accounts You take the oath (electronically, in USOS), apply for the appointment of your supervisor and create an account in the Polish Scholarly Bibliography (PBN) linked to ORCID and POLON. The discipline council appoints your supervisor within 3 months at the latest. PBN/ORCID/POLON accounts: 14 days; supervisor request: 1 month; oath: 30 days
  3. First year Individual research plan (IPB) Together with your supervisor you prepare the IPB — the topic and aim of the research, hypotheses, methods, a schedule, and the form and submission date of the thesis. The plan is approved by the head of the College. Within 12 months of starting your training
  4. Every semester and year Courses, teaching and annual assessment Each semester you submit a course plan for approval by the head of the College (270 hours in total, including at least 4 lecture courses with an exam and 2 seminars). You teach as part of your professional practice, hold office hours (1 hour a week) and help with the Faculty's organisational work (up to 10 hours a year). Course plan: within 2 weeks of the start of the semester; annual assessment documents: by 15 September
  5. Halfway through Mid-term evaluation A three-member committee (with at least one member from outside the University) evaluates the progress of your IPB based on your report, your supervisor's opinion, and a presentation followed by a discussion. The result is either positive or negative; a negative result means removal from the list of doctoral students. Committee meetings: 1–30 September; report at least 30 days before
  6. Date set in the IPB Thesis submission Your training ends when you submit the thesis, with your supervisor's positive opinion, together with an application to start the doctoral degree proceedings. In justified cases the deadline can be extended by up to 2 years in total.
  7. After submission Reviews and public defence The Mathematics Discipline Council appoints the reviewers. After at least two positive reviews — a public defence and a resolution conferring the doctoral degree. Defence announcements are published on the PhD defences page.

FAQ

Do I need a supervisor before I apply?

Yes. To apply, a candidate must first have a prospective supervisor, who must hold a habilitation or the title of professor. Contact details of the mathematicians are in the staff directory. Details are on the Admissions page.

What is the entrance exam like?

It is a written exam. Candidates receive 12 problems (in Polish or English — their choice), and the committee grades the 6 best solutions, each on a scale of 0–6 points. The scope covers courses taught in mathematics degree programmes, in particular analysis I–III, linear algebra and algebra I, differential equations, elements of metric topology and set theory, and probability and statistics. Problems from previous years.

Can I apply without a master's degree?

In cases justified by research achievements of the highest quality — yes, if you hold a bachelor’s degree or have completed the third year of a long-cycle master’s programme. You need an opinion from an independent researcher justifying the high level of your achievements; the admissions committee decides whether you may take the exam.

Can I do my doctorate in English?

Admission is conducted in Polish or English, and the exam problems are prepared in both languages. Courses in the College are taught in Polish or English, and the mid-term evaluation is held in Polish or English.

How much is the doctoral stipend?

At least 37% of a professor’s salary until the month in which the mid-term evaluation takes place, and at least 57% afterwards. The amount for each academic year is set by the Rector. The stipend can be received for a maximum of 4 years in total. More on the Stipends and grants page.

Can I work at a university during my training?

A doctoral student may not be employed as an academic teacher or researcher (with the exceptions set out in Article 209(10) of the Law on Higher Education and Science). The head of the College must be notified immediately of any such employment.

What if I cannot submit my thesis on time?

The head of the College may extend the submission deadline by up to 2 years in total — for example because of illness, care of a child under 4 or of a sick family member, disability, or the need for long-term research. The request is filed after the mid-term evaluation and before the end of the training period. During an extension, once 4 years have passed since the start of training, no stipend is paid.

Where can I find the list of doctoral students and defence announcements?

The Institute’s doctoral students are listed in the staff directory (group “PhD students”). Defence notices, theses and reviews are published on the PhD defences page.
