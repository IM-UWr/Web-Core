Exams and diploma

How to complete a degree in mathematics: supervisor and thesis topic, the APD system, bachelor's and master's exams, dates and past exam papers.

A guide to completing your degree in mathematics: choosing a supervisor and thesis topic, submitting the thesis in the APD system, the bachelor’s and master’s exams, dates and past exam papers. The formal rules — the Diploma Theses Committee, thesis assessment, the final degree grade and answers to frequent questions — are described in the Diploma procedure.

Next diploma exam: 15 February 2027. Detailed dates for registration and for the steps in the APD system are announced by the Institute before each exam session. For an idea of how a session runs, see the 2025/26 schedule.

Path to the diploma

Bachelor’s studies (first cycle)

The rules depend on the curriculum you follow — which curriculum applies to me?

ProjectTeam specialisation projectTeam project; in the theoretical mathematics specialisation — Individual project
Thesisbachelor’s thesis written under a supervisorno diploma thesis

Previous curriculum:

  1. Agree on a bachelor’s thesis topic with a supervisor and submit it using Form E — see Supervisor and thesis topic.
  2. Write the thesis following the Faculty guidelines and, once your supervisor accepts it, submit it in the APD system.
  3. Pass all courses and take the written bachelor’s exam (February, June or September).

Curriculum from 2026/27: students do not write a diploma thesis. After passing semester 6 and all other courses they take a written diploma exam covering the courses: Mathematical Analysis 1–3, Linear Algebra 1–2, Algebra 1, Differential Equations 1 and Probability 1. Students of the theoretical mathematics specialisation prepare, within the Individual project (semester 6) and under a chosen staff member, a piece of work similar in character to a diploma thesis.

Master’s studies (second cycle)

  1. Semester 2 — Master’s seminar 1 helps you choose the subject of your thesis and introduces the rules for writing it.
  2. End of semester 2 or beginning of semester 3 — through individual consultations you choose a supervisor, agree on the subject of the thesis and report your choice using Form E.
  3. Semesters 3–4 — you write the thesis (it takes two semesters). In Master’s seminar 2 (semester 4) you report on your progress; students of theoretical mathematics and actuarial and financial mathematics take this seminar in a form set by their supervisor.
  4. After passing all courses and receiving a positive thesis grade — the master’s exam: first the written part, then the oral part.

In the curriculum from 2026/27 a positively graded thesis gives 12 ECTS and the passed diploma exam 4 ECTS.

Students of Artificial Intelligence in Data Science (SIAD) — information on the team project and the diploma exam is on the SIAD programme page.

Supervisor and thesis topic

At the Institute students choose the supervisor of their diploma thesis (bachelor’s or master’s) themselves. This lets you match the subject of the thesis to your interests, and writing the thesis is based on individual cooperation with the supervisor. You can obtain a topic in two ways:

  1. choose it from the list of thesis topics approved by the Diploma Theses Committee — some topics require additional knowledge, so it is best to contact the staff member who proposed the topic before choosing it;
  2. agree on a topic with your future supervisor during individual consultations — such a topic must be approved by the Diploma Theses Committee.

If you have trouble finding a supervisor, contact the coordinator of your specialisation (contacts on the specialisation pages).

Master’s seminars introduce a chosen area of mathematics and develop the skills of speaking and writing about mathematics, but they do not replace the supervisor — no thesis topics are offered at the seminar, and the thesis is directed by the supervisor.

Reporting the topic — Form E

As soon as you have agreed on the subject with your supervisor, fill in Form E. A topic that is not on the list will be passed to the Diploma Theses Committee for approval.

Changing the topic requires the approval of the Diploma Theses Committee, and changing the supervisor requires the Dean’s consent; details are in the Diploma procedure.

Writing the thesis

The thesis must follow the diploma thesis guidelines adopted by the Faculty Council; the commentary of the Diploma Theses Committee explains them with examples (both in Polish). The key rules:

  • Aim, knowledge, own contribution. The thesis must have a clearly defined aim (research, application, or the author’s own treatment of a known subject), require mathematical knowledge and skills at university level, and contain a clearly identified contribution of the author.
  • Bachelor’s vs master’s. A bachelor’s thesis may be a compilation, provided its “added value” compared with existing texts is easy to identify. A master’s thesis requires solving the stated problem independently (e.g. proving a theorem, analysing data, designing and implementing an algorithm).
  • Project. A thesis may take the form of a project, but a computer program alone is not enough — a theoretical treatment of the problem is required.
  • Joint thesis. Several students may write a joint thesis; its topic is approved separately, and the introduction must specify the contribution of each author. Joint master’s theses are not recommended.
  • Language. With the Dean’s consent the thesis may be written in a foreign language (in practice English or Russian — this depends on finding a supervisor and a reviewer who know the language); it must then include a title and an abstract in Polish.
  • Form. TeX is recommended; pages are numbered starting from the title page. The thesis must cite the literature (at least one item), and material taken from sources must be clearly marked.

Before the exam every thesis is checked with anti-plagiarism software.

Submitting the thesis in APD

Diploma theses are submitted only electronically in the Diploma Theses Archive (APD) — students do not submit a paper copy to the Faculty Office (Rector’s orders no. 111 and 112/2022).

  1. Once your supervisor has accepted the thesis, e-mail a request to open the APD system for you, stating your specialisation, the thesis title and the names of the supervisor and the reviewer (the list of students with their supervisors and reviewers is published by the teaching office). Addresses: krystyna.piekarska@uwr.edu.pl — bachelor’s theses, ewa.ratajczak@uwr.edu.pl — master’s theses.
  2. By the date given in the session schedule, enter the thesis details and upload the thesis file in APD.
  3. The supervisor approves the thesis in APD and sends it for the anti-plagiarism check; then the supervisor and the reviewer post their reviews and grades in APD — before the exam.
  4. At least 3 days before the exam, settle all obligations towards the University, including the library e-clearance, following the University Library instructions.

The full requirements for admission to the diploma exam (§ 62 of the Study Regulations) are described in the Diploma procedure.

Bachelor’s exam

The bachelor’s exam is written; it is organised by the Diploma Exams Committee chaired by the Vice-Dean for Mathematics Education.

  • Requirements: all courses of the curriculum passed.
  • What to bring: student ID or identity card and a pen. Calculators, phones and other aids are not allowed.
  • Duration: 4 hours — 2 hours for the test and 2 hours for the open problems.
  • Problems: 12 test problems and 6 open problems, the same for all specialisations.
  • Scope: basic mathematical analysis, algebra, differential equations and probability — bachelor’s exam syllabus (required notions, skills and sample problems; in Polish).

Each test problem contains 4 short-answer questions; 0, 1, 2, 3 or 4 correct answers give 0, 1, 3, 6 or 10 points respectively. Each open problem is worth up to 20 points. The maximum is 240 points (120 for the test and 120 for the open problems).

Master’s exam

The master’s exam has a written and an oral part; it is organised by the Diploma Exams Committee chaired by the Vice-Dean for Mathematics Education. The written part tests knowledge in your specialisation, and the oral part concerns the subject of your master’s thesis.

Written part

  • Requirements: all courses of the curriculum passed and a positive grade for the master’s thesis.
  • What to bring: student ID or identity card and a pen. Calculators, phones and other aids are not allowed.
  • Duration: 1 hour 45 minutes.
  • Problems: 4, different for each specialisation, covering the core knowledge of the specialisation; 32 points in total.

Oral part

  • Requirements: a passed written part.
  • Format: about 25–30 minutes, including a presentation of about 10 minutes on the results of the thesis (slides or a short talk at the blackboard). Then the three-member committee — the supervisor, the reviewer and the chair — asks questions related to the subject of the thesis.
  • What to bring: student ID or identity card and, if you use one, your presentation. The committee provides a laptop and a screen, but it is better to have your own laptop and, just in case, the presentation on a USB stick.

Exam result

The master’s exam grade is ¾ of the written part grade plus ¼ of the oral part grade. A positive grade in the oral part is required to pass. If you fail the oral part, the Dean sets a second date — without repeating the written part.

Dates

Diploma exams are held three times a year: in February, June and September. You must register for the written exam within the set period; at the same time you upload the thesis to APD. Next exam: 15 February 2027.

Best diploma thesis competition

The Faculty of Mathematics and Computer Science runs a competition for the best diploma theses, including the categories of the best bachelor’s thesis and the best master’s thesis in mathematics. Faculty graduates whose theses defended in the given period received a very good grade may take part; the authors of the best theses present them at the competition final. Rules, application deadlines and results of past editions: the competition on the Faculty website.
