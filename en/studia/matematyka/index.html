About

Mathematics at the University of Wrocław combines a solid foundation in pure mathematics with applications — from data analysis and modelling to insurance and teaching. Classes are taught by researchers of the Mathematical Institute, one of the strongest mathematical centres in Poland.

Studies are full-time and taught in Polish. After the bachelor’s degree you can continue with the two-year master’s programme.

A+ category Mathematics at the University of Wrocław holds the highest research rating in Poland — you learn from active researchers.
The Wrocław school of mathematics The tradition of Hugo Steinhaus and Edward Marczewski, who built Wrocław mathematics after the war.
Support from day one A preparatory course before the first year, a tutoring room run by senior students and a year coordinator.
Student clubs and exchanges Two student research clubs, summer schools, and Erasmus+, MOST and ISEP exchanges.

How the studies work

Bachelor's studies

  1. Semesters 1–2 Foundations Core courses, including mathematical analysis, linear algebra and an introduction to mathematics. The year coordinator and the tutoring room help you settle in. Teaching specialisation: declare by 31 December (1st semester) · Formularz C1
  2. Semesters 3–4 Broadening and choosing a specialisation Further core courses and the first electives. At the end of the 4th semester you choose a specialisation. Declare your specialisation by 30 June (4th semester) Formularz C2
  3. Semesters 5–6 Specialisation and bachelor's diploma Specialisation courses, a team specialisation project and a bachelor's thesis. Studies end with a written bachelor's exam (February, June or September).

Master's studies

  1. Semester 1 Choosing a master's specialisation In the master's programme you follow your chosen specialisation from the start. Declare your specialisation by 15 October Formularz C3
  2. Semesters 2–3 Seminar and choosing a supervisor The master's seminar develops speaking and writing about mathematics. At the end of the 2nd or beginning of the 3rd semester you choose a supervisor and a thesis topic. Report your supervisor by 31 October (summer admission) or 31 March (winter admission) Formularz E
  3. Semester 4 Master's thesis and exam Master's thesis and exam: a written part covering the specialisation and an oral part with a presentation of the thesis.

Specialisations

AD

Data analysis

Mathematical statistics, linear models, machine learning and methods for analysing large data sets, together with programming in R, Python and SQL.

For whomFor those who want to combine mathematics with practical data analysis.

CareersFor example data analyst (after the first cycle) or Data Scientist (after the second cycle), including in industry and start-ups that rely on data science tools.

More about this specialisation

Coordinator: prof. dr hab. Małgorzata Bogdan, dr Grzegorz Wyłupek Department of Statistics

MS

Applied mathematics

Modelling deterministic and random phenomena: differential equations, stochastic processes, numerical methods and simulations, combined with data analysis and the natural sciences.

For whomFor those who enjoy differential equations and probability, want to combine mathematics with programming and computation, and are interested in applications in the natural sciences.

CareersWork in sectors requiring high competence, critical thinking and non-standard problem solving — e.g. industry and innovative start-ups.

More about this specialisation

Coordinator: prof. dr hab. Grzegorz Karch Department of Differential Equations

MAF

Actuarial and financial mathematics

Probability and stochastic processes applied to insurance and financial markets: pricing financial instruments, life and non-life insurance mathematics, risk assessment.

For whomFor those interested in risk, insurance and financial markets.

CareersThe financial and insurance sector, e.g. as an actuary or a risk specialist.

More about this specialisation

Coordinator: dr Marek Arendarczyk Department of Probability Theory

TEOR

Theoretical mathematics

Organised, in-depth knowledge of key mathematical theories — from algebra and analysis, through topology and logic, to probability — and fluency in rigorous proofs.

For whomFor those who want to learn mathematics through theory and proofs and are thinking about advanced study and research.

CareersPreparation for advanced master's studies and research; a natural talent pool for university teaching and research staff.

More about this specialisation

Coordinator: dr hab. Jan Dymara, prof. UWr

Mathematics teaching

Mathematics combined with psychological, pedagogical and didactic training and school placements. After the master's degree you are fully qualified to teach mathematics in schools.

For whomFor future mathematics teachers.

CareersTeaching mathematics in all types of schools (full qualifications after the master's degree).

More about this specialisation

Coordinator: dr Elżbieta Kalinowska, prof. dr hab. Jacek Świątkowski Mathematics Education and Outreach Unit

After graduation

Mathematics teaches precise thinking and problem solving, so graduates find work in many places: data analysis and IT, banking and insurance, research centres, schools and universities.

The best master’s graduates can continue at the doctoral school. Employment and salary data for mathematics graduates of the University of Wrocław are published by the national Graduate Tracking System (ELA).

FAQ

When do I choose my specialisation?

In the bachelor’s programme at the end of the 4th semester (form C2, by 30 June). The teaching specialisation is declared in the 1st semester (form C1, by 31 December). In the master’s programme — in the 1st semester (form C3, by 15 October).

Which curriculum applies to me?

It depends on when you started: from 2026/27 the new curriculum applies, between 2022/23 and 2025/26 — the 2022 curriculum. Pick your year in the “Curriculum” section.

Can I withdraw from a course after registration closes?

Yes, but only once per degree — by 30 November (winter semester) or 30 April (summer), using form A1 (bachelor’s) or A2 (master’s).

I don't meet a course's prerequisites. What can I do?

In the bachelor’s programme, submit form B1 by 1 October and register only after a positive decision. In the master’s programme, the lecturer’s consent is enough.

Can I take courses at the Institute of Computer Science?

Yes, if places are available. You need an account in the Institute of Computer Science registration system — see Classes and office hours.

Where can I get help in my first year?

In the tutoring room (room 202), where senior students help with problems, from the year coordinator and on the First year page.

When are the diploma exams held?

Three times a year: in February, June and September. Rules, scope and dates: Exams and diploma.
